THE 70/30 RULE: WHAT AI CAN AND CAN'T DO IN RAILS DEVELOPMENT

After months of building production Rails applications with AI assistance, I've discovered a critical truth: AI handles 70% of the code, but humans handle 100% of the decisions. Here's why you need an experienced pilot at the controls.

AI is a force multiplier, not a replacement. The same AI that makes a senior developer 5x more productive can lead a junior developer into a security nightmare. The difference isn't the AI—it's the pilot.

The hype around AI coding tools is deafening. "AI will replace developers!" "No-code is the future!" "Anyone can build software now!"

I've spent the last year testing this thesis by building production Rails applications using Claude, Cursor, and other AI tools. The reality is more nuanced—and more interesting—than either side claims.

THE 70%: WHERE AI EXCELS

Let me be clear: AI is extraordinarily good at certain types of development work. When I say AI handles 70% of the code, I mean it generates or substantially assists with the following:

CRUD Operations

Creating controllers, models, views, and forms for standard data operations is AI's sweet spot. Describe what you need in natural language, and you get correct, idiomatic Rails code: 

"Create a RESTful controller for Projects with index, show, 
new, create, edit, update, and destroy actions. Projects 
belong to a Client and have a name, description, status 
enum, and due_date."

AI generates this perfectly. What used to be 30 minutes of typing is now 2 minutes of review.

Database Migrations

Tell AI what you need, get a correct migration every time: 

class AddStatusToProjects < ActiveRecord::Migration[8.0]
  def change
    add_column :projects, :status, :integer, default: 0, null: false
    add_index :projects, [:client_id, :status]
  end
end

Test Writing

Describing expected behavior and having AI write tests is incredibly efficient. I describe what I want to test, AI writes comprehensive RSpec tests that would take 20+ minutes manually.

View Templates & CSS

ERB templates, Tailwind classes, responsive layouts—AI's output is often better than what I'd write because it's seen millions of examples. "Style this form with a card layout, proper spacing, and a prominent submit button" produces clean, working code.

Documentation & Boilerplate

README files, inline comments, API documentation, configuration files—all the repetitive-but-necessary work that slows down development.

THE 30%: WHY YOU NEED AN EXPERIENCED PILOT

Here's where the "anyone can code with AI" narrative falls apart. The 30% that AI cannot handle well is the 30% that makes the difference between working software and a security breach, between maintainable code and technical debt, between a product that scales and one that collapses.

Architecture Decisions

Should you use schema-per-tenant or row-level security for multi-tenancy? REST or GraphQL? Sidekiq or Solid Queue? PostgreSQL or MySQL?

AI will give you pros and cons, but it won't make the decision. It doesn't know your team's experience level, your scale requirements, your existing infrastructure, or your business constraints. I've seen AI confidently recommend patterns that would fail spectacularly at scale.

Security Review

This is the most critical gap. AI-generated code often has security vulnerabilities that look correct but aren't.

Consider this common scenario:

# AI might generate this
def show
  @user = User.find(params[:id])
end

# But it should be this
def show
  @user = current_tenant.users.find(params[:id])
end

The first version leaks data across tenants. AI doesn't understand your authorization model unless you explicitly tell it every time. And you won't always remember to tell it.

Every controller action, every query, every user input handling needs a human with security experience reviewing it. This isn't optional.

Business Logic Validation

AI doesn't know your business. It doesn't know that German invoices require specific VAT calculations, that your client has an unusual approval workflow, or that certain data combinations should be impossible.

When I built DATEV export functionality recently, AI gave me a starting point. I then spent hours validating against official documentation because getting German accounting data wrong has legal consequences.

Performance at Scale

AI optimizes for correctness, not performance. It doesn't automatically consider:

  • What happens when this table has 1 million rows?
  • Should this query be eager-loaded?
  • Does this need a database index?
  • Should this run in a background job?

I review every database query in AI-generated code. N+1 queries slip through constantly.

User Experience Design

AI can implement features but doesn't design experiences. The flow from registration to first value, error messages that help instead of frustrate, progressive disclosure of complexity—these require human empathy and design thinking.

THE AI PILOT ANALOGY

Think of AI coding tools like a Tesla on autopilot. The car can handle 90% of highway driving perfectly. But you still need a licensed driver who:

  • Knows when to take over
  • Understands what the car can't see
  • Can handle edge cases and emergencies
  • Takes responsibility for outcomes

The same applies to AI-assisted development. The AI handles the routine work. An experienced developer:

  • Makes architecture decisions the AI can't
  • Catches security vulnerabilities the AI misses
  • Validates business logic the AI doesn't understand
  • Optimizes for performance the AI ignores

AI HANDLES WELL (70%)

  • CRUD controllers & views
  • Database migrations
  • Test writing
  • CSS/Tailwind styling
  • Documentation
  • Boilerplate code
  • Standard patterns

REQUIRES EXPERIENCE (30%)

  • Architecture decisions
  • Security review
  • Business logic validation
  • Performance optimization
  • UX design
  • Gem/library selection
  • Edge case handling

THE PRODUCTIVITY MULTIPLIER

Here's the key insight: AI is a multiplier, not a replacement.

A senior developer with AI assistance isn't just 70% faster. They're 3-5x more productive because they can focus entirely on the high-value work—the 30% that actually matters.

What takes a traditional development team 4 months, I deliver in 3-4 weeks. Not because AI is magic, but because:

  • 15 years of experience tells me which architecture decisions to make
  • I know what security vulnerabilities to look for
  • I understand German business requirements and compliance
  • I can spot N+1 queries and performance issues

AI handles the typing. Experience handles everything else.

THE DANGER ZONE

Where teams get into trouble with AI-assisted development:

  1. Trusting AI output without review. AI code looks correct. It often isn't.
  2. Asking AI for architecture decisions. It will answer confidently. It will often be wrong.
  3. Skipping security review. AI doesn't understand your authorization model.
  4. Assuming AI-generated tests are sufficient. AI tests what you describe, not edge cases you didn't think of.
  5. Letting junior developers fly solo with AI. They don't know what they don't know.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOUR PROJECT

If you're building a production Rails application in 2025, the question isn't whether to use AI tools—they're too powerful to ignore. The question is: who's at the controls?

The economics have shifted dramatically. You no longer need a 5-person team for 4 months. One senior developer with AI assistance can deliver faster, with fewer communication gaps, and often with better quality.

But that developer needs to be experienced enough to know what AI gets wrong. Otherwise, you're building a house of cards.
